## SPLIT THE FRAME, AND MAKE THE SPLIT THE DESIGN

Not two flat halves doing different things. The division itself is composed.

- **GEOMETRY IS ASYMMETRIC.** Thirds. A narrow vertical strip against a wide field.
  A diagonal. An irregular tear. A circular inset punched through. Equal halves are
  the least interesting option on the table -- use them only deliberately.
- **THE SEAM IS DRAWN.** Say what the border is MADE of: a thick keyline, a torn
  paper edge, a black gutter, a chromatic gap where the channels separate, a
  hairline of blown highlight, a strip of the underlying texture showing through.
- **THE PANELS DISAGREE.** Different grade -- one cold, one sodium. Different
  distance -- macro against wide. Different TIME -- one panel in slow motion while
  the other runs at speed. Different place entirely. Agreement wastes the device.
- **THE DIVISION CAN MOVE.** A seam that slides across the shot. A panel that grows
  and crowds the other out. One strip that multiplies into four. The layout is not
  obliged to hold for the whole shot.
- **LET SOMETHING BLEED.** A hand, smoke, a light, a letterform crossing the seam --
  for that moment the division is a lie, and that is the shot people remember.
- State how many panels, where the seam sits, what each panel holds, and what the
  border is made of. A split frame described only as "split screen" renders as two
  flat halves.
